## Summary

This note records a useful neuroscience-adjacent research signal: reports that congenital cortical blindness appears to be associated with extremely low or absent observed schizophrenia diagnosis in available population-level evidence.

The key relevance is not blindness itself. The important mechanism is the proposed link between early visual-cortex development, predictive processing, sensory weighting, and the stability of reality interpretation.

The reported pattern suggests that early cortical sensory architecture may strongly influence how the brain assigns weight to ambiguous signals, prediction errors, salience, and internally generated interpretation.

---

## Why this matters for Verrell's Law

Verrell's Law treats retained information as a weighting influence on future selection.

This research direction is relevant because schizophrenia is often discussed through predictive-processing and Bayesian-brain models: the brain continuously predicts incoming sensory information and updates its model when prediction error occurs.

If weak, noisy, or ambiguous signals are overweighted, interpretation may destabilise. Coincidences can appear too meaningful, internally generated thoughts can be misattributed, and reality parsing may become less stable.

That maps cleanly onto the broader Verrell's Law vocabulary:

```text
prior state / retained information
→ weighting of present signal
→ altered interpretation
→ changed future selection / behaviour
```

This does not prove Verrell's Law. It does, however, support the broader principle that weighted interpretation systems can strongly shape downstream reality-modelling and behavioural outcomes.

---

## Why this matters for Collapse Aware AI

Collapse Aware AI is an engineering implementation of governed, memory-weighted behavioural selection.

The same general architecture appears in a different domain here:

```text
sensory input
→ predictive model
→ signal weighting
→ salience assignment
→ interpretation
→ behavioural response
```

For CAAI, the analogous risk is not schizophrenia. The relevant engineering risk is unstable behavioural interpretation caused by poor weighting, poor memory control, noisy recall, false salience, or runaway continuity.

This reinforces the need for:

- Governor-controlled weighting
- Truth-Hedge Bias (THB)
- Weighted Meaning Layer (WML)
- Corrective Recall Layer (CRL)
- Dynamic Weighted Moment Revision (DWMR/WMRL)
- salience control
- uncertainty residue tracking
- bounded memory influence
- explicit non-proof disclaimers

A badly governed predictive system can overinterpret weak signal. A well-governed system must know when to downweight noise, preserve uncertainty, and avoid forcing premature collapse.

- that blindness should be induced or sought
