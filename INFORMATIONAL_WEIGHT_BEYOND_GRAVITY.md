# Informational Weight Beyond Gravity

**Working Note — Verrell’s Law Research**  
**Author designation: VMR Core**  
**Organisation: Inappropriate Media Limited**  
**Status: Developing conceptual framework — open to testing, revision and falsification**

---

## Abstract

The word **weight** is normally associated with gravitational force. This working note uses the term more broadly and operationally.

Information can influence physical systems without possessing independent mass or acting as a separate force. It can direct energy, constrain accessible states, alter entropy, modify transition probabilities and preserve the consequences of earlier events.

This note defines **informational weight** as the measurable influence of retained or organised information on what a system can do next.

The proposal does not require information to exist independently of matter, energy or fields. Information must be physically represented. Its influence arises through the organisation, history and present configuration of its carrier.

Established physics already connects information with thermodynamics, computation and work extraction. The developing question is whether informational organisation can be treated as a distinct, measurable state variable that improves prediction beyond coarse descriptions such as total mass, total energy or temperature alone.

The aim is therefore not to announce a new force. It is to define and test how retained physical organisation changes future possibilities.

---

## 1. Central proposition

Two systems may contain the same kinds of matter and similar total energy while behaving differently because their internal states are organised differently.

A charged capacitor and a discharged capacitor may contain the same components.

A working computer and a damaged computer may contain nearly the same materials.

Two brains may receive the same stimulus while producing different responses because their structures and histories differ.

The relevant difference is not necessarily the amount of matter present. It is the arrangement, accessibility and retained organisation of the system.

This motivates the following operational definition:

> **Informational weight is the measurable influence that retained or organised physical information exerts on the accessible future states, transition probabilities or behaviour of a system.**

The phrase does not imply gravitational mass.

It describes how strongly prior or present organisation changes what can happen next.

---

## 2. Information is not separate from its carrier

Information does not normally appear without a physical representation.

It may be encoded through:

- electrical charge;
- magnetic orientation;
- molecular sequence;
- chemical concentration;
- neural connectivity;
- mechanical arrangement;
- electromagnetic field variation;
- optical state;
- or quantum state.

The physical carrier contains the relevant matter and energy.

The information lies in distinguishable relations, arrangements and correlations within that carrier.

A radio transmission is physical electromagnetic radiation. Its information is encoded through organised variation in amplitude, frequency, phase, timing, polarisation or spatial structure.

The signal and its organisation are distinguishable concepts, but they are not independent substances.

This framework therefore does **not** propose that abstract information floats separately from physical reality.

It proposes that organisation is physically consequential.

---

## 3. Why total energy is not a complete description

Total energy alone does not tell us everything a system can do.

The distribution and organisation of energy matter.

Two systems with the same total energy may differ in:

- entropy;
- free energy;
- available work;
- stability;
- internal correlations;
- permitted transitions;
- response to external input;
- and future evolution.

A wound spring and a relaxed spring may contain the same material but have different capacities to perform work.

A charged battery and a depleted battery may have nearly identical mass but radically different functional possibilities.

A functioning machine and a scrambled machine may contain the same parts while possessing different accessible behaviours.

These are not violations of conventional physics.

They show that **state organisation is physically relevant and cannot be replaced by total quantity alone**.

Informational weight is intended to describe that relevance where retained organisation changes future selection.

## 4. Forms of informational weight

Informational influence may be expressed through several overlapping physical channels.

### 4.1 Causal weight

Information has causal weight when one encoded state changes which later event occurs.

Examples include:

- a control instruction switching a circuit;
- a genetic sequence affecting development;
- a stored rule changing software behaviour;
- a remembered event altering a later choice;
- or a sensor reading redirecting a machine.

The informational state does not need to supply most of the energy used by the resulting process.

It changes which pathway becomes active.

### 4.2 Thermodynamic weight

Information has thermodynamic relevance when informational operations affect work, heat or entropy.

Logically irreversible operations, particularly resetting a memory without preserving its previous logical state, are subject to thermodynamic constraints.

This does not mean that every act of computation must dissipate the same fixed amount of energy.

It means that information processing is implemented physically and cannot be separated completely from thermodynamics.

### 4.3 Structural weight

Earlier events may alter the physical arrangement of a system.

Examples include:

- neural plasticity;
- magnetic storage;
- molecular configuration;
- material deformation;
- circuitry;
- receptor distribution;
- and gene regulation.

The altered structure changes how the system responds later.

### 4.4 Radiative weight

Information may be encoded and transmitted through electromagnetic radiation.

Relevant properties include:

- frequency;
- amplitude;
- phase;
- timing;
- polarisation;
- coherence;
- spatial pattern;
- and correlation.

Radiation supplies physical energy and momentum.

Its organisation determines what information can be transmitted and what effect a receiver may produce.

### 4.5 Chemical weight

Biological and chemical systems retain information through molecular structure, concentration and reaction pathways.

Examples include:

- DNA;
- hormones;
- neurotransmitters;
- epigenetic regulation;
- cellular signalling;
- and immune memory.

### 4.6 Probabilistic weight

Retained information may change the probability distribution over later outcomes.

In behavioural systems, this may appear as:

- memory-weighted selection;
- learned preference;
- expectation;
- conditioned response;
- or risk adjustment.

In physical and computational systems, it may appear as:

- state-dependent transition;
- feedback;
- adaptive control;
- Bayesian updating;
- or altered boundary conditions.

These channels can operate together.

A biological memory may involve structural, chemical, electrical, energetic and probabilistic effects simultaneously.

## 5. Established foundations in information thermodynamics

The relationship between information and physical processes is not merely philosophical.

Several major results establish a rigorous connection.

### Szilárd’s engine

Leo Szilárd’s 1929 thought experiment showed how information about a particle’s position could be used within a feedback process to extract work from a thermal system.

The lesson is not that abstract knowledge creates energy.

The engine converts available thermal energy into work because acquired information allows the system to select an effective operation.

Information directs the process.

### Landauer’s principle

Rolf Landauer argued in 1961 that logically irreversible information erasure has a minimum thermodynamic cost.

For an idealised one-bit reset performed at temperature \(T\), the minimum average heat dissipation approaches:

\[
Q_{\min} = k_B T \ln 2
\]

where \(k_B\) is Boltzmann’s constant.

The principle applies to logically irreversible transformations under appropriate physical conditions. It should not be simplified into the claim that every change of information automatically produces this exact heat cost.

### Bennett and reversible computation

Charles Bennett developed the connection between logical reversibility and thermodynamic reversibility.

His work showed that computation need not be intrinsically dissipative at every logical step. In principle, logically reversible operations can avoid the Landauer cost associated with irreversible information loss.

The unavoidable cost appears when information is irreversibly discarded or reset within a complete physical process.

### Experimental verification

Experiments including Bérut and colleagues’ 2012 work measured heat dissipation approaching the Landauer bound in a controlled one-bit memory system.

These results support a precise conclusion:

> Information processing is physical, and logically irreversible manipulation of information is constrained by thermodynamics.

They do not establish that information is an independent force or substance.

## 6. Direction of energy rather than supply of energy

Information frequently influences large processes while supplying very little of their energy.

A key does not provide the energy required to open a powered security door.

A control voltage does not supply the full energy moved through an industrial circuit.

DNA does not provide the metabolic energy used to build an organism.

A software instruction does not supply the electricity consumed by a machine.

In each case, information changes:

- permission;
- timing;
- pathway;
- sequence;
- constraint;
- or selection.

This produces an important asymmetry:

> The energetic magnitude of a signal may be small while its causal influence is large.

Informational weight can therefore be understood as **selection leverage**: the degree to which organised information redirects a larger physical process.

## 7. Retained informational consequence

A system does not need to preserve a readable transcript of an event for that event to remain causally active.

Earlier events may leave consequences through:

- altered connectivity;
- charge distribution;
- physical deformation;
- chemical concentration;
- receptor sensitivity;
- magnetic alignment;
- conditioned response;
- or modified control parameters.

The system may not explicitly record what happened.

Its present state is nevertheless different because the event occurred.

This is described here as **retained informational consequence**.

> An earlier event remains active when it changes the present organisation from which later behaviour or physical transition proceeds.

This provides a physical account of history dependence without requiring conscious memory.

---

## 8. Path dependence

A system is path-dependent when its present or future behaviour depends not only on its current external input, but also on the sequence of states through which it arrived.

Examples include:

- hysteresis in magnetic materials;
- deformation history;
- biological development;
- learning;
- immune response;
- financial and social systems;
- adaptive control;
- and memory-influenced behaviour.

Two systems may appear similar under a coarse measurement while responding differently because their retained histories differ.

In Verrell’s Law terms, the current state contains more than the immediate stimulus.

It contains accumulated consequences of prior transitions.

---

## 9. Relationship to Verrell’s Law

Verrell’s Law proposes that future behaviour is selected from a present state already shaped by retained prior information.

The process can be represented as a recurring sequence:

1. An event changes a system.
2. Part of that change persists.
3. The retained state influences a later transition.
4. That transition creates further consequences.
5. The new consequences become part of the next state.
6. The cycle continues.

Informational weight names the influence carried from one stage into the next.

A compact formulation is:

> **The past carries informational weight wherever its retained physical consequences alter the possibilities of the present.**

This interpretation does not require a separate memory field, unknown particle or gravitational effect.

It requires measurable history dependence.

## 10. A more precise experimental definition

The phrase informational weight becomes scientifically useful only when connected to measurable variables.

For a defined system, let:

- \(H\) represent relevant retained history;
- \(X\) represent the presently measured physical state;
- \(Y\) represent a later observable outcome.

A system displays measurable historical informational influence when:

\[
P(Y \mid X, H) \neq P(Y \mid X)
\]

That is, knowledge of retained history improves prediction of the later outcome beyond the chosen present-state measurements alone.

This does not prove that history acts independently of physical state.

It may instead reveal that the chosen measurement of the present state is incomplete and that history identifies physically relevant organisation not yet captured by \(X\).

This distinction is essential.

A positive result would show one of two things:

1. retained history carries predictive information that the current measurement has missed; or
2. the current physical model omits a relevant state variable associated with that retained history.

Either outcome is scientifically useful.

## 11. The developing hypothesis

The established claim is:

> Organised physical information can affect work, entropy, control, transition and behaviour.

The developing hypothesis is narrower than claiming a new force:

> Retained informational organisation may be quantifiable as an effective state variable that improves prediction of later physical or behavioural outcomes beyond aggregate measurements such as total energy, mass, temperature or immediate stimulus.

Possible signatures include:

- history-dependent transition rates;
- altered coherence;
- persistent correlations;
- delayed release patterns;
- state-dependent radiation;
- changed extractable work;
- or reproducible behavioural selection bias.

The relevant effect must remain physically implemented.

The framework does not assume that organisation acts outside matter or energy.

---

## 12. Radiation as a research channel

Electromagnetic radiation is a possible carrier and measurement channel for informational organisation.

A system’s prior state may influence:

- emission timing;
- spectral distribution;
- phase relationships;
- coherence;
- polarisation;
- pulse structure;
- or spatial correlation.

A radiative experiment would therefore ask:

> Does retained system history produce a reproducible difference in emitted or transmitted radiation after known present-state variables have been controlled?

A positive result would not automatically reveal a new force.

It could indicate:

- an unmeasured physical state;
- persistent internal correlation;
- delayed relaxation;
- structural memory;
- or another conventional mechanism.

The value of the experiment would lie in detecting and modelling that retained influence, not prematurely labelling it exotic.

---

## 13. Biological inheritance boundary

Biological inheritance is carried primarily through molecular, cellular and developmental mechanisms.

These include:

- DNA sequence;
- gene regulation;
- cellular environment;
- developmental signalling;
- and, in limited contexts, epigenetic inheritance.

This framework does not claim that personality, ADHD, memory or inherited traits are transmitted through an unidentified electromagnetic or gravitational field.

Electromagnetic activity may participate in biological operation, but that is different from claiming that inherited behavioural information is carried between generations through radiation.

Different channels must not be conflated.

---

## 14. Dark matter and dark energy boundary

There is currently no established evidence that dark matter or dark energy consists of stored information, memory or informational weight.

Dark matter is inferred primarily from gravitational behaviour associated with galaxies, clusters and large-scale structure.

Dark energy is the name given to whatever is driving the observed accelerated expansion of the universe within current cosmological models.

The underlying nature of both remains unresolved.

Their unresolved status does not itself support an informational explanation.

A future theory may connect information, gravity and spacetime more deeply, but this working note makes no claim that informational weight currently explains either dark matter or dark energy.

---

## 15. What would count as evidence?

A useful theory must generate measurements that distinguish it from vague description.

Possible evidence would include:

1. **History-dependent prediction**  
   Retained history reliably improves prediction after defined present-state variables are controlled.

2. **Matched-state behavioural divergence**  
   Systems exposed to the same immediate stimulus produce systematically different outcomes because of experimentally controlled prior states.

3. **Thermodynamic difference**  
   Different informational organisations produce reproducible differences in available work, dissipation or entropy under clearly specified conditions.

4. **Radiative signature**  
   Prior organisation produces a repeatable spectral, temporal, coherence or correlation pattern.

5. **Transition-rate change**  
   Retained history shifts the probability of later state transitions.

6. **Formal compression**  
   A model containing an informational-state variable predicts observations more accurately or economically than a model using aggregate physical quantities alone.

7. **Independent replication**  
   The effect survives blinded testing and replication by independent researchers.

A result must be compared with conventional explanations before any stronger interpretation is considered.

---

## 16. Falsification and failure conditions

The framework would be weakened if:

- retained history provides no predictive improvement once relevant physical state variables are measured;
- claimed effects vanish under controlled replication;
- all observed differences are explained by ordinary measurement error or uncontrolled variables;
- informational terminology adds no measurable variable or predictive value;
- or the concept is repeatedly redefined after negative results.

A valid experiment must state in advance:

- the system being studied;
- the information-bearing state;
- the retained-history variable;
- the physical or behavioural outcome;
- the controls;
- the expected result;
- and the result that would count against the hypothesis.

The phrase **informational weight** must never become a label applied automatically to every unexplained effect.

---

## 17. Current boundary of the claim

This document does **not** claim that:

- information possesses independent gravitational mass;
- information is a new fundamental force;
- organisation acts outside its physical carrier;
- dark matter is stored information;
- dark energy is universal memory;
- radiation is conscious;
- every physical structure is memory;
- or unexplained effects automatically support Verrell’s Law.

The present claim is:

> Information is physically instantiated, and retained organisation can alter the accessible states, transition probabilities and behaviour of physical systems.

The developing research proposition is:

> Informational weight may be measurable as the predictive or causal influence of retained physical organisation on later outcomes.

That proposition remains open to formalisation and experimental testing.

---

## Working formulation

> **Information does not need gravitational mass to carry weight. It carries operational weight wherever retained physical organisation changes what a system can do next.**

A stricter experimental version is:

> **Informational weight is present when a defined retained state produces a reproducible change in later outcome, and the size of that change can be measured against an explicit baseline.**
