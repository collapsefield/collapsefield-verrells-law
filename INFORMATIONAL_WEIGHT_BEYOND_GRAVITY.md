## Abstract

The term **informational weight** does not need to refer exclusively to gravitational mass.

Information may produce measurable physical consequences through energy transfer, entropy, heat, radiation, state transition, probability weighting and causal influence.

This working note proposes that information should be examined not merely as abstract description, but as organised difference capable of changing the future behaviour of a physical system.

Under this interpretation, informational weight may appear through several physical channels rather than through one universal force.

The strongest current scientific position is not that information has been proven to possess independent mass, but that information is physically instantiated and that changes in informational organisation can produce measurable energetic and thermodynamic consequences.

The unresolved question is whether informational organisation has detectable effects beyond those currently attributed to the material or energetic carrier in which it is encoded.

---

## 1. The central proposition

Information changes what physical systems can do.

A system containing one arrangement of information may behave differently from an otherwise similar system containing another arrangement.

The difference may appear as:

- altered energy flow;
- different heat production;
- different entropy change;
- modified radiation patterns;
- changed transition probabilities;
- altered response selection;
- or different amounts of extractable work.

This suggests that the word **weight** may be used in a broader operational sense.

> Informational weight is the measurable influence that retained or organised information exerts on the later state, behaviour or energetic evolution of a system.

This is not yet a claim that information constitutes an independent substance.

It is a proposal that information may possess measurable consequence wherever organisation changes physical outcome.

---

## 2. Weight does not require gravity

In ordinary language, weight usually means force caused by gravity.

Within this research note, informational weight refers to a broader class of measurable effects.

Possible forms include:

### 2.1 Causal weight

Information has causal weight when one informational state changes which later event occurs.

For example:

- a stored instruction changes machine behaviour;
- a remembered threat changes animal behaviour;
- a genetic sequence changes biological development;
- a control signal changes an electrical circuit;
- a prior observation changes a later decision.

The information affects future selection.

### 2.2 Thermodynamic weight

Information has thermodynamic weight when changes to stored or processed information produce measurable heat, entropy or work requirements.

The physical handling of information is constrained by thermodynamics.

Information is not computationally free.

### 2.3 Energetic weight

Information has energetic weight when its organisation changes the amount, direction or usefulness of available energy.

Two systems may contain similar total energy while differing substantially in the work they can perform because their internal organisation differs.

### 2.4 Radiative weight

Information may be carried through electromagnetic radiation.

It may be encoded through:

- frequency;
- phase;
- amplitude;
- polarisation;
- timing;
- pulse structure;
- spatial pattern;
- and correlation.

Radiation can therefore act as a carrier through which informational organisation produces remote physical effects.

### 2.5 Probabilistic weight

Information may alter the probability distribution over possible future outcomes.

A stored condition, observation or memory may make one later transition more likely than another.

In behavioural systems, this appears as weighted selection.

In physical systems, it may appear as constraint, feedback or altered state transition.

## 3. Information and its physical carrier

Information does not normally appear without a physical representation.

It may be represented through:

- electrical charge;
- magnetic orientation;
- molecular structure;
- neural connectivity;
- electromagnetic radiation;
- mechanical configuration;
- quantum state;
- chemical concentration;
- or spatial arrangement.

The physical carrier contains energy and obeys physical law.

The information is found in the distinguishable organisation of that carrier.

This distinction matters.

A radio signal carries information because its electromagnetic field has been structured in a way that can be detected and interpreted.

The radiation is physical.

The information lies in the pattern.

The open question is whether the pattern itself produces measurable consequences that cannot be fully reduced to a simple total-energy description.

---

## 4. Thermodynamic information

Information theory and thermodynamics are already connected.

Erasing, transforming and processing information can require work and generate heat.

This establishes an important principle:

> Changing informational state may produce measurable physical cost.

This does not prove that abstract information possesses independent mass.

It does show that information cannot always be treated as physically irrelevant description.

The structure of a system affects its thermodynamic possibilities.

A more organised state may contain different accessible futures than a disordered state, even where the total matter present remains similar.

Information may therefore influence not only description, but physical capability.

---

## 5. Radiation as an informational mechanism

Radiation provides a clear example of information moving through physical space.

Electromagnetic radiation can transport:

- energy;
- momentum;
- timing information;
- structural information;
- control instructions;
- environmental measurements;
- and encoded memory.

The information does not float separately from the radiation.

It is expressed through the radiation’s organised properties.

This creates a possible research direction:

> Could retained informational organisation produce distinctive radiative signatures, coherence patterns or energy-distribution effects that reveal more than total emitted power alone?

Such an effect would not necessarily imply a new force.

It might appear as:

- unusual spectral structure;
- persistent correlation;
- delayed energy release;
- altered coherence;
- non-random temporal patterning;
- or state-dependent emission.

The measurable object would be radiation, while the relevant cause would be informational organisation.

## 6. Retained information and physical consequence

A system may retain the consequence of earlier events without storing a readable transcript of those events.

Examples include:

- altered neural connectivity;
- structural deformation;
- magnetic alignment;
- chemical concentration;
- receptor sensitivity;
- genetic regulation;
- conditioned response;
- modified circuitry;
- and changed environmental state.

The prior event remains active through the present organisation of the system.

This may be described as **retained informational consequence**.

The system does not need to consciously remember the event.

Its present behaviour is different because the event occurred.

In Verrell’s Law terminology:

> The past remains physically active where earlier information has altered the present structure from which future behaviour is selected.

---

## 7. Informational weight as constraint

Information may operate by reducing or restructuring the set of accessible future states.

A key, password or control instruction does not need to supply all the energy used by a machine.

It changes which energetic pathway becomes available.

This suggests an important distinction between:

- supplying energy;
- and directing energy.

Information frequently performs the second role.

A small informational input may redirect a much larger energetic process.

Examples include:

- a control signal opening a high-power circuit;
- DNA directing large-scale biological construction;
- a neural impulse triggering muscular action;
- software determining the operation of physical machinery;
- or a sensor reading changing an automated system’s behaviour.

The informational signal may be energetically small while being causally decisive.

That difference is itself a form of informational weight.

---

## 8. Relationship to Verrell’s Law

Verrell’s Law proposes that present state is shaped by retained prior information and that future selection occurs from that weighted state.

Under this interpretation:

1. earlier events alter system organisation;
2. altered organisation changes later response;
3. later response produces further informational consequence;
4. those consequences become part of the next state;
5. the process continues recursively.

Informational weight therefore refers to the persistence of prior organisation in present selection.

It does not require that information possess literal gravitational mass.

It requires that prior information remain capable of changing later physical or behavioural outcome.

A working formulation is:

> Information carries weight wherever retained organisation changes the energy, entropy, radiation, probability or behaviour of a later state.

## 9. Possible physical channels

Informational influence may be expressed through several overlapping channels.

### Channel A — Structural

Earlier information alters physical arrangement.

Examples:

- neural pathways;
- crystal structure;
- magnetic storage;
- molecular configuration;
- circuitry.

### Channel B — Thermodynamic

Information alters work, heat or entropy requirements.

Examples:

- computation;
- erasure;
- sorting;
- error correction;
- feedback control.

### Channel C — Radiative

Information is encoded and transmitted through field variation.

Examples:

- light;
- radio;
- infrared;
- neural electromagnetic activity;
- optical signalling.

### Channel D — Chemical

Information is encoded through molecular concentration or reaction pathways.

Examples:

- hormones;
- neurotransmitters;
- gene expression;
- cellular signalling.

### Channel E — Probabilistic

Information alters which future state is selected.

Examples:

- behavioural bias;
- adaptive control;
- Bayesian updating;
- state-dependent transition;
- feedback systems.

These channels need not be mutually exclusive.

A biological memory, for example, may simultaneously involve structural, chemical, electrical, energetic and probabilistic effects.

## 10. Dark matter and dark energy boundary

It is presently unproven that dark matter or dark energy consists of information.

Dark matter is inferred mainly from gravitational effects.

Dark energy is inferred mainly from the accelerated expansion of the universe.

Their underlying physical nature remains unresolved.

Informational explanations may eventually be proposed, but no established evidence currently demonstrates that either phenomenon is stored information, memory or informational weight.

The defensible position is:

> Unknown cosmological effects may eventually reveal deeper relationships between information, spacetime, energy and organisation, but current evidence does not identify dark matter or dark energy as informational storage.

This research note therefore does not claim that informational weight already explains those phenomena.

It identifies them only as areas where unknown physical structure remains open to investigation.

---

## 11. What would count as evidence?

A serious theory of informational weight must produce measurable predictions.

Possible evidence could include:

1. A reproducible energetic difference between systems with equivalent material content but different informational organisation.

2. A measurable heat or entropy effect not fully explained by known processing operations.

3. A radiative signature correlated with retained informational structure.

4. A persistent change in transition probabilities caused by prior information after ordinary transient effects are removed.

5. A measurable difference in extractable work caused by informational organisation.

6. A state-dependent physical effect that survives replication across independent laboratories.

7. A formal model connecting informational organisation to energy, entropy or field behaviour.

8. A prediction that distinguishes informational weight from conventional material, energetic or statistical explanations.

Without such evidence, informational weight remains a conceptual framework rather than a confirmed physical law.

---

## 12. Falsification boundary

The proposal would be weakened if:

- all apparent informational effects reduce completely to already measured energy differences;
- no reproducible consequence remains after controlling for physical carrier state;
- informational organisation produces no predictive improvement;
- claimed radiative effects disappear under blinded replication;
- or the term “information” cannot be defined operationally for the experiment.

The model must not become unfalsifiable by redefining every physical effect as informational weight after the event.

A valid theory must state in advance:

- what is being measured;
- which informational variable is changing;
- which physical consequence is predicted;
- and what result would count against the model.

---

## 13. Current boundary of the claim

This document does **not** claim:

- that information is proven to possess independent mass;
- that information is a new fundamental force;
- that information explains dark matter;
- that information explains dark energy;
- that radiation is itself conscious;
- that all physical structure is memory;
- or that informational organisation acts independently of a physical carrier.

The present claim is narrower:

> Information is physically represented, and changes in informational organisation can alter energy flow, entropy, radiation, state transition and future behaviour.

The deeper hypothesis is:

> Informational organisation may possess measurable physical significance that is not adequately described by total matter or total energy alone.

That hypothesis remains open.

> **Information does not need gravitational mass to carry weight. It carries weight wherever its retained organisation changes the physical possibilities of what happens next.**
