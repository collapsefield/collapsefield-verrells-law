## 1. Purpose

This note sets out a clean information-first formulation of Verrell’s Law.

It defines the relationship between information, memory, observation, measurement, field-mediated influence, collapse as realised selection, and Collapse Aware AI as an engineering application.

The framework does not require consciousness to be treated as a separate substance. It also does not require quantum collapse as its default mechanism.

---

## 2. Compact Formulation

Verrell’s Law proposes that systems with retained prior state do not collapse future behaviour from present input alone.

> Future selection is biased by retained information from prior states.

A fuller formulation:

> Any system that retains information as memory-like influence will carry bias forward. Present input enters a field of possible outcomes already shaped by prior state, salience, repetition, decay, and observation. The realised outcome is produced by present conditions interacting with weighted history.

In this framework, collapse means realised selection.

---

## 3. Information and Memory

Memory is treated as retained information capable of influencing future processing.

> Information becomes memory when it leaves a trace capable of biasing future selection.

Momentary information may pass through a system without persistence. Memory-like information persists, repeats, stabilises, or couples back into the system strongly enough to affect later outcomes.

Working chain:

1. Information is registered.
2. Some information is reinforced, repeated, or weighted.
3. Weighted information becomes memory-like influence.
4. Memory-like influence biases future selection.

---

## 4. Consciousness Position

In this framework, consciousness is treated as the experienced interface of high-density, recursive, memory-weighted information processing.

A concise position:

> Consciousness is the experienced interface of organised information, memory, attention, prediction, and salience being processed at high density.

Consciousness is therefore not the foundation of Verrell’s Law. It is one possible high-level expression of the deeper rule:

> Retained information shapes future selection.

The framework does not claim that consciousness has been proven in artificial systems, or that consciousness requires quantum computation.

---

## 5. Observer and Measurer Weighting

Observation is not treated as passive.

An observer or measuring system samples, selects, reinforces, records, and weights some information more strongly than other information.

Principle of observer-weighted salience:

> An observer or measuring system assigns differential weight to information through attention, repetition, importance, measurement, interaction, and contextual relevance. Information that receives greater weight is more likely to stabilise, propagate, or return as influence on future selection.

The key point:

> Not all information matters equally.

Some information becomes high-weight because it is repeated, important, surprising, corrected, measured, or strongly connected to prior state.

---

## 6. Field and Electromagnetic Position

Verrell’s Law allows field-coupled informational hypotheses, but it does not depend on proving that complete memories are stored in electromagnetic fields.

Safe formulation:

> Electromagnetic or field activity may carry, reflect, or modulate structured information. Such activity becomes memory-like only when it is retained, reinforced, propagated, or coupled back into matter strongly enough to bias later selection.

Model:

1. Field activity may carry structured information.
2. Some field patterns may become coherent, repeated, or reinforced.
3. Reinforced field activity may couple back into material systems.
4. If later selection is measurably shifted, the field activity has acted as a memory-like bias.

This keeps the field hypothesis open while preserving scientific caution.

---

## 7. Packeted Influence

Some information may remain low-influence until it becomes sufficiently coherent, reinforced, or propagated.

Public-safe description:

> Information may remain low-influence until it forms a coherent packet of retained influence capable of biasing a later selection event.

The important idea is thresholded influence:

> Some traces are too weak to matter. Some become strong enough to bias collapse.

Software mapping:

- weak signal: ignored or decayed
- repeated signal: reinforced
- high-salience signal: promoted
- persistent signal: becomes memory-like
- governed signal: allowed, damped, or blocked

---

## 8. Collapse as Realised Selection

In Verrell’s Law, collapse should usually be read as realised selection.

It can refer to:

- a behavioural response selected by an agent
- a memory reconstruction becoming dominant
- an interpretation winning over competing interpretations
- a software system selecting one candidate output
- a physical system settling into one realised state

Safe formulation:

> Collapse is the narrowing of a possibility field into one realised outcome under weighted conditions.

Quantum-level hypotheses may be explored separately, but they are not required for the operational law.

## 10. Human Observability

For the framework to matter in software, a human user should be able to observe memory-weighted influence over time.

In a Phase-2 software system, this means the user should be able to see that:

- prior interaction changes future behaviour
- repeated preferences gain influence
- high-salience moments are weighted more strongly
- unresolved threads remain behaviourally relevant
- the system can explain what influenced a response
- the governor can allow, damp, or block memory influence

A clean software test:

> Same model, same prompt, different retained history, different governed response.

A stronger test:

> Same prompt with memory off, memory on, governor off, and governor on, with logs showing how retained state changed selection.

---

## 11. Testability and Failure Conditions

A valid operational test should include:

- matched present input
- controlled retained history
- declared memory or weighting terms
- memory-off baseline
- fixed selection rules
- repeatable measurement criteria
- logs showing whether history affected selection

The claim fails in a target domain if retained history produces no measurable difference over a matched present-state model, or if the supposed memory effect can be fully explained by ordinary present-state input alone.

> If history does not measurably matter under controlled conditions, the claim fails in that domain.

---

## 12. Claim Boundaries

This framework does not claim that:

- consciousness has been fully explained
- artificial systems are conscious
- complete memories are stored in electromagnetic fields
- quantum collapse is required
- personal synchronicity is scientific proof
- field-coupled informational effects have been independently validated
- Collapse Aware AI proves the full natural-law hypothesis

It does claim, as a working and testable framework, that:

- retained information can influence future selection
- memory can be modelled as future-influencing retained information
- observation and measurement can weight what becomes important
- not all information carries equal future influence
- collapse can be modelled as realised selection
- governed memory influence is a useful engineering pattern

---

## 13. Summary

Verrell’s Law is best understood as a framework for memory-weighted selection.

Its core is:

> Information becomes memory when it persists as influence. Memory becomes bias when it affects future selection. Observation and measurement determine what information gains enough weight to matter. Collapse is the realised outcome of that weighted field.

Collapse Aware AI applies this principle in software by making retained history influence future behaviour under governor control.

That is the clean bridge between the law and the middleware.
