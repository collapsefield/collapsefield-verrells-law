## 1. Core Claim

Consciousness, in its coherent human form, is not a finished object present fully formed at birth. It appears to emerge through the ordering of sensory input, biological drives, memory, and feedback into stable, usable bias.

A sharper form:

> Conscious agency emerges when biological awareness becomes organised into memory-weighted predictive control.

This does **not** claim that newborns have no awareness. It claims that early awareness is unstable, reflex-dominated, and not yet organised into the kind of coherent self-directed frame ordering seen in mature consciousness.

---

## 2. Infant Consciousness and the Formation of Bias

A newborn or very young infant does not begin as a blank machine, but it also does not begin with a stable adult-like self-model.

The infant already has inherited biological structure:

- reflexes;
- hunger and comfort drives;
- pain responses;
- startle reactions;
- attachment bias;
- rhythm and voice sensitivity;
- primitive body regulation;
- early sensory-motor loops.

However, the infant has not yet accumulated enough structured experience to produce stable, predictive behavioural control.

In plain terms: the infant has biological awareness, but not yet coherent agency.

The early system is dominated by:

```text
Low continuity memory
Low motor confidence
Weak self-model
High uncertainty
High noise
Reflex-dominated response
Limited prediction stability
```

Through repeated sensory feedback, caregiver interaction, body correction, pain/comfort mapping, voice recognition, face recognition, emotional salience, and motor trial-and-error, the infant begins to build weighted memory structures.

Over time, repeated events become weighted moments. Weighted moments become bias. Bias stabilises prediction. Prediction stabilises behaviour.

This gives a clean developmental framing:

> The brain does not act from emptiness. It acts from bias. In infancy, bias is mostly biological and reflexive; with experience, bias becomes memory-weighted, predictive, and eventually self-directed.

## 3. Time as Experienced Frame Ordering

The same principle applies to subjective time.

External clock time may continue regardless of an individual observer's state. The claim here is narrower and more defensible:

> Experienced time emerges from ordered conscious memory.

When a person is knocked unconscious, sedated, concussed, or otherwise loses coherent awareness, the external world continues. But the person's conscious chain of frames is interrupted.

Upon waking, the person often has to reconstruct:

```text
Where am I?
What happened?
What time is it?
What day is it?
What was I doing before this?
How long was I out?
```

This suggests that conscious time is not simply a passive internal clock. It is an active ordering process involving memory, attention, sensory input, context, and continuity.

The subjective structure of unconsciousness is often not:

```text
waiting in darkness for ten minutes
```

but rather:

```text
frame before blackout -> discontinuity -> frame after waking
```

The missing interval is not experienced as normal time. It is inferred afterwards from clocks, other people, bodily state, injuries, location, context, and memory reconstruction.

This produces the working claim:

> Conscious time is reconstructed continuity. When the memory-frame chain is interrupted, the person does not experience the missing interval directly; they infer it after coherence returns.

## 4. Link Between Infant Development and Unconsciousness

The infant case and the unconsciousness case point to the same underlying structure.

```text
Infant:
low ordered memory -> unstable self/control

Unconscious adult:
interrupted memory-frame chain -> unstable time/self-location

Awake adult:
ordered memory + sensory input + predictive bias -> coherent self in time
```

Both examples suggest that consciousness depends on active information ordering, not merely on raw biological activity.

The infant has biological activity and sensory input, but lacks mature ordered memory and stable predictive self-control.

The unconscious adult has a mature brain and stored memory, but the active frame-ordering process is temporarily broken.

In both cases, coherent consciousness depends on continuity.

## 5. Verrell's Law Interpretation

Within the Verrell's Law framework, this note supports the broader idea that observation and experience are not neutral. They are shaped by memory, bias, salience, and prior exposure.

The suggested mapping is:

```text
Consciousness = active information ordering
Memory = stored frame/context
Bias = weighted influence from prior frames
Experienced time = sequential frame binding
Unconsciousness = collapse or interruption of active frame binding
Recovery = reassembly of the continuity chain
Agency = stabilised memory-weighted prediction applied to action
```

This aligns with the principle that reality, as experienced by an observer, is parsed through weighted frames rather than received as a clean, unfiltered stream.

The observer does not merely witness frames. The observer's memory and bias help order them.

## 7. Boundaries and Non-Claims

This note does **not** claim:

- that babies have no consciousness;
- that external physical time stops during unconsciousness;
- that memory alone explains all consciousness;
- that Verrell's Law is experimentally proven by infant development or unconsciousness;
- that AI systems are conscious because they use memory or state.

The narrower claim is:

> Coherent conscious agency and experienced time appear to depend on ordered memory continuity, with biological drives and sensory input becoming progressively weighted into predictive control.

That claim is harder to attack and more useful for future formalisation.

## 8. Possible Testable Angles

Potential future research directions:

1. **Infant development:** compare increasing motor control and attention stability with memory weighting, predictive modelling, and sensory feedback integration.

2. **Concussion and blackout recovery:** study how people reconstruct time after unconsciousness, especially what cues restore self-location first.

3. **Anaesthesia reports:** compare subjective absence of time with memory interruption and post-recovery reconstruction.

4. **AI continuity tests:** compare stateless generation against memory-weighted governed systems when asked to preserve identity, priorities, and temporal context across sessions.

5. **Frame-order disruption experiments:** investigate how degraded memory ordering affects perceived time, agency, and self-continuity.

## 9. Working Summary

The current theory can be compressed as follows:

> Consciousness becomes coherent when biological awareness is organised into memory-weighted frame ordering. Time, as experienced by the observer, emerges from the continuity of those ordered frames. When the chain is weak, as in infancy, agency is unstable. When the chain is interrupted, as in unconsciousness, subjective time disappears or becomes reconstructive. When the chain is stable, memory and bias generate a coherent self moving through time.

Or shorter:

> Consciousness is not just awareness. It is ordered continuity.
