## 1. Summary

This note explores a possible extension of Verrell's Law into what may be called **consciousness-wave behaviour**: the idea that conscious experience may involve coherent, wave-like organisation across biological, electromagnetic, informational, and observational systems.

Electromagnetic field theories of consciousness, including proposals such as McFadden's CEMI theory and Pockett's field-based model, remain minority positions within mainstream neuroscience. This note treats them as research directions worth examining, not as established consensus.

The claim is not that every wave pattern is conscious.

The claim is more careful:

> Conscious systems may represent a specialised, higher-order case of a broader natural principle: local interactions forming coherent global behaviour through shared fields, resonance conditions, recursive feedback, and memory-weighted prior states.

This aligns with the central claim of Verrell's Law:

> Prior states are not always neutral history. Under certain conditions, retained information may bias future selection.

In this context, **memory**, **field structure**, **harmonic resonance**, and **observation** are treated as possible contributors to collapse-like selection across complex systems.

Verrell's Law proposes that retained information from prior states may alter the probability landscape of future outcomes.

## 3. Why Wave Behaviour Matters

Nature repeatedly shows wave-like organisation when many local units interact through a shared medium.

Examples include:

- neural oscillations
- cardiac and respiratory rhythms
- chemical waves
- bacterial collective patterns
- synchronised animal behaviour
- electromagnetic field interactions
- phase-locking across coupled oscillators
- attention-dependent neural coherence

These systems do not require a central controller.

They can form structured global behaviour through local rules, feedback, gradients, resonance, and constraint.

This is important because consciousness may not require a single central "seat." It may instead involve a coherence condition across interacting subsystems.

## 6. Frame-Based Time and Conscious Experience

Verrell's Law treats time as a sequence of organised frames rather than a simple universal flow.

Under this view, conscious experience may arise through the ordering of state transitions:

```text
state -> observation -> collapse-like selection -> updated state -> next frame
```

The perceived continuity of consciousness may therefore be produced by coherent frame-to-frame integration.

A conscious system does not merely process isolated moments. It carries weighted prior states forward.

This creates continuity.

In Verrell's Law terms:

```text
present state != present input alone

present state = current input + retained bias from prior states
```

This may explain why two systems receiving similar inputs can diverge if their prior histories differ.

## 7. Harmonic Resonance and Memory-Weighted Selection

Harmonic resonance may be relevant because it provides a possible mechanism for selective amplification.

If two or more processes are rhythmically compatible, energy or information transfer may become more efficient.

In a Verrell's Law framing, resonance may act as a biasing condition:

```text
if current stimulus resonates with prior memory structure:
    selection probability increases
else:
    selection probability weakens or remains neutral
```

This gives a possible physical analogy for memory-weighted selection.

A retained prior state may not need to "push" the future directly. It may instead tune the system so that certain future states become easier to select.

This matters for consciousness because memory may not simply be stored content. It may also be a tuning condition.

## 11. Testable Research Directions

This note suggests several possible research directions:

### 11.1 Neural Coherence

Can changes in attention, memory recall, or emotional salience be measured as shifts in neural synchrony or coherence?

Possible signals:

- EEG rhythm changes
- phase-locking
- alpha/theta/gamma coupling
- coherence between brain regions
- changes during memory recall vs passive perception

### 11.2 Heart-Brain Coupling

Can cardiac rhythm, breathing rhythm, and neural oscillation alignment influence subjective coherence or memory recall?

Possible signals:

- heart-rate variability
- breath pacing
- EEG synchrony
- meditation state comparison
- recall accuracy under different rhythmic states

### 11.3 Memory as Tuning

Can repeated exposure make later recognition or selection easier through measurable resonance-like effects?

Possible signals:

- faster recall
- altered attention bias
- changed EEG response
- emotional salience amplification
- priming under controlled conditions

### 11.4 Field Sensitivity

Can weak electromagnetic changes influence perception, attention, or recall under controlled conditions?

This must be approached carefully.

The aim is not to assume exotic effects, but to test whether field conditions can measurably bias complex biological state selection.

### 11.5 Artificial Analogue

Can artificial systems reproduce memory-weighted divergence?

Collapse Aware AI already explores this engineering question:

```text

+ different memory state
= different behavioural selection
```

This provides a computational analogue for the wider Verrell's Law claim.
