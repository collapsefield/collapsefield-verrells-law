# Light-Biased Collapse: Retained-State Bias, Thresholds, and Testable Analogies

This note is part of the Verrell’s Law research archive. It should be read as a **research analogy and hypothesis note**, not as completed physics proof and not as a dependency of Collapse Aware AI.

In 2025, researchers at NC State reported light-controlled tuning of quantum-dot bandgaps in a microfluidic system. The useful pattern for Verrell’s Law is not that this proves a universal collapse field. The useful pattern is narrower and safer:

> External interaction can alter the state of a system, and that altered state can constrain or bias later outcomes.

That is the retained-state mechanism in its cleanest public form.

---

## Why It Matters

A bandgap is a threshold-like property. If illumination changes that threshold, the system’s later behaviour is not determined by the present input alone; it is also shaped by the retained state produced by prior interaction.

For Verrell’s Law, the relevant bridge is:

```text
Interaction / illumination
        ↓
State change in the substrate
        ↓
Changed threshold / response profile
        ↓
Altered future outcome distribution
```

This is a physical analogy for retained-state bias. It does **not** establish that memory is stored as a non-local field, and it does **not** prove that software, brains, and quantum materials share one identical mechanism.

## Phase-2 Research Directions

Possible future research directions include:

- controlled bias-response tests using optical or photonic systems

These should be treated as proposed research paths, not validated conclusions.
