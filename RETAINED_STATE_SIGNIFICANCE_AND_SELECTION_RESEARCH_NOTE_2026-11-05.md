# Verrell’s Law — Significance, Retained State and Subsequent Selection
**Public research direction | 5 November 2026**

**Originator:** Marcos Verrell Moss Ross (M.R.)  
**Research status:** Dated statement of an active investigation; not a report of completed empirical findings  
**Relationship to existing work:** Extends the experimental questions of the established retained-state selection framework without altering its canonical mathematics.

## Research proposition

Verrell’s Law investigates whether past interactions leave persistent, measurable changes that influence how later candidate outcomes are weighted and selected under otherwise matched present conditions.

A current research question concerns **unequally consequential interactions**: whether some events, because of their independently assessed importance, persistence, contextual relevance or reliability, exert a stronger or differently structured influence on subsequent weighting and selection than otherwise comparable events.

This question does not require a particular physical carrier, universal field, observer-controlled quantum collapse or a claim about consciousness. Its purpose is to turn a broad intuition about retained history into observable, contestable predictions.

## Mathematical foundation

For a fixed candidate set, let the present-state utility be `U(y_i;S_t,O_t)` and the retained-history contribution be `B(y_i;M_t)`. The canonical selection family is:

```math
z_i=U(y_i;S_t,O_t)+\lambda B(y_i;M_t),\qquad
P_i=\frac{e^{z_i}}{\sum_j e^{z_j}}.
```

Equivalently, with a fixed baseline `p_{0,i}` and independently specified score `R_i`:

```math
p_{\lambda,i}=\frac{p_{0,i}e^{\lambda R_i}}{\sum_j p_{0,j}e^{\lambda R_j}}.
```

The exact pairwise identity is:

```math
\Delta\log\mathrm{odds}(a,b)=\lambda(R_a-R_b).
```

This mathematical identity holds under the stated finite-score, common-positive-support assumptions. It does **not** on its own establish a unique empirical mechanism; an unconstrained score could fit any positive target distribution on the common support.

## What the November 2026 reviews established

Private mathematical reviews and synthetic model-comparison exercises during 1–4 November 2026 have:

- checked the algebraic structure and its identifiability boundaries;
- clarified why retained information must be distinguished from cueing, retrieval, activation, lasting updating and actual influence on selection;
- identified conditions where adding information that is relevant but directionally neutral may produce different model predictions;
- compared plausible aggregation and retrieval families against established averaging, Bayesian updating, reinforcement learning, instance-based sampling and normalisation;
- refined the requirements for independent score definition, held-out prediction, intervention and comparison with conventional history-sensitive explanations.

These are mathematical and simulated results, not new human or physical observations. No distinctive cross-domain rule has yet been independently confirmed.

## New investigative emphasis: event significance and predictive influence

The next phase will examine how to define the **significance of prior interactions** independently of the later outcome. An event being personally, functionally or physically consequential is not automatically evidence that it influences a subsequent selection; that influence must be measured.

The working questions are:

1. Can a predeclared measure of interaction significance, considered alongside persistence and reliability, improve prediction of later choices over a present-state-only baseline?
2. When two histories differ in their significant events but are matched on declared present variables, does a fixed retained-state score predict the direction and size of subsequent selection differences?
3. Do independently assessed event significance, access to retained information and candidate-direction compatibility make separable contributions?
4. Are any observed effects explained as well or better by established attention, learning, memory retrieval, Bayesian, sampling or normalisation models?
5. Can any predictive relationship transfer across independently tested settings without redefining its scoring rule after seeing the results?

These are **research questions**, not adopted modifications to the original law. In particular, the existing illustrative weights `w_j=s_j d_j q_j` retain their established meanings: salience, persistence and reliability. No new aggregation formula, retrieval rule or threshold is announced here.

## What would count as progress

The programme requires independently fixed measurements and rules, prospective observations, logged null and comparison conditions, and analyses that allow predictions to fail. First test one specifically declared contrast in a feasible system, then replicate it with fresh data and independent scrutiny before making broader claims.

A positive finding that merely reproduces established history-sensitive behaviour would be informative but would not establish a new law. A stronger result would be a predeclared quantitative prediction that conventional alternatives do not explain equally well.
