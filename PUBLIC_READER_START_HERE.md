## 1. The shortest explanation

Verrell’s Law is a retained-state selection framework.

It asks whether two otherwise matched systems can diverge because they carry different retained histories, and whether that difference can be measured, predicted and changed by intervention.

The non-trivial claim is not simply that memory can affect behaviour. The retained-state variable must be defined before confirmatory testing and must survive comparison against a present-state baseline and simpler alternative explanations.

In compact form:

```text
same present condition
+ different retained history
→ measurable selection difference?
```

If no repeatable, directional and predictive difference survives the declared controls, the claim is not supported in that tested regime.

### Retained-state selection specification

The primary general mathematical references remain:

The first-order relationship is expressed as:

```math
\Delta L = \lambda\Delta R
```

where `ΔL` is a candidate-relative change in selection log-odds and `ΔR` is a preregistered, normalized retained-state compatibility difference.

### Physics-facing technical extension

The September 2026 paper adds a separate open-system formulation:

Ordinary non-Markovianity is not itself evidence for that stronger claim.

## 3. What the framework does not claim

The current research position does not require or establish:

- a universal electromagnetic memory field;
- non-local personal memory storage;
- consciousness-caused quantum collapse;
- a new fundamental force;
- information acting without a physical carrier;
- one identical mechanism across software, cognition, biology and physics;
- software behaviour as proof of a natural law.

Older speculative material is preserved for provenance but does not override these boundaries.

---

## 4. What makes a useful test

A useful retained-state test should, where applicable:

- hold the present comparison condition within a declared tolerance;
- vary retained history in a controlled or randomised way;
- freeze scoring / weighting rules before confirmatory analysis;
- compare against a declared baseline or conventional model;
- use held-out prediction rather than fit quality alone;
- include intervention or ablation on the retained state;
- specify what result would count as failure.

Measurement limitations are legitimate constraints, but they are not evidence for an undetected mechanism.

---

## 5. Physics-facing evidence ladder

The September 2026 technical edition uses a cumulative ladder:

1. **Formal identifiability**
2. **Controlled history divergence**
3. **Intervention and ablation**
4. **Metrological and thermal convergence**
5. **Independent replication**

A sophisticated equation is not experimental evidence. A sensor detection without causal intervention does not establish the stronger claim. Replication must reproduce the preregistered effect and its intervention pattern, not merely another example of ordinary memory or non-Markovian dynamics.

---

## 6. Engineering boundary

A deliberately engineered retained-state selector can be tested for conformance, repeatability and causal influence inside that engineered system.

That is legitimate software evidence.

It is not, by construction, independent confirmation of a physical, biological or universal mechanism. Engineering and scientific claims are therefore kept separate.
