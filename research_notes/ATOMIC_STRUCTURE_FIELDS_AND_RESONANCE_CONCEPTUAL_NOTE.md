## 1. Purpose

This note records a conceptual line of inquiry linking three established areas of physics:

1. atomic structure;
2. electromagnetic and quantum interactions between matter;
3. frequency-dependent response and resonance.

The purpose is not to claim that atomic physics proves Verrell’s Law, consciousness physics, non-local memory, or an electromagnetic memory field.

The narrower purpose is to preserve a potentially useful research question:

> **If apparently solid matter is governed at microscopic scales by distributed quantum states, electromagnetic interaction, permitted transitions and frequency-dependent response, can resonance or frequency-sensitive coupling ever provide a measurable substrate-specific modulation of retained-state effects?**

That question is open. It requires measurement, not assertion.

---

## 2. The “Mostly Empty Atom” Picture — Useful but Incomplete

A typical atom has a characteristic size on the order of an ångström, while its nucleus is on the femtometre scale. In geometric terms, the nucleus therefore occupies an extremely small fraction of the atom’s overall spatial extent.

This often leads to the phrase “atoms are mostly empty space.”

That phrase is useful only as a rough geometric intuition. It becomes misleading if it suggests that an atom is a tiny hard nucleus surrounded by a literal empty void and a hard electron shell.

Quantum mechanically:

- electrons are described by quantum states rather than classical planetary orbits;
- electron probability density can extend throughout regions around the nucleus;
- atomic structure is governed by quantised states and interactions;
- the region outside the nucleus is not a set of ordinary macroscopic tunnels through which bulk matter can simply pass.

The important distinction is therefore:

> **Atoms are spatially sparse at the nuclear scale, but they are not classically hollow objects with unobstructed gaps.**

---

## 3. Why Matter Feels Solid

When two macroscopic objects meet, their nuclei do not normally collide directly with one another.

The resistance associated with ordinary solidity emerges from quantum and electromagnetic physics, particularly:

- electrostatic interaction between charged constituents;
- the structure of electron states in neighbouring atoms;
- the Pauli exclusion principle for identical fermions;
- the energy cost of forcing electronic wavefunctions into increasingly confined or incompatible states.

The Pauli exclusion principle is not an extra classical repulsive force. It is a restriction on the allowed quantum states of identical fermions. Together with electromagnetic interactions and quantum kinetic effects, it is central to atomic structure and the stability and volume of ordinary matter.

This gives a more accurate conceptual picture than “hard balls touching hard balls”:

```text
Nuclei
   +
Distributed electronic quantum states
   +
Electromagnetic interaction
   +
Quantum-state constraints
   ↓
Stable atoms, molecules and macroscopic structure
```

The solidity experienced at the human scale is therefore an emergent macroscopic consequence of microscopic interactions and state constraints.

## 4. Frequency Is a Real Physical Variable — but Not a Universal Explanation

Frequency enters atomic and molecular physics in several legitimate ways.

Atoms and molecules possess discrete energy structures. Molecular systems can also rotate and vibrate. Electromagnetic radiation carries energy according to frequency, and a system may absorb or emit strongly when the supplied energy matches an allowed transition or mode.

Very broadly:

- rotational molecular transitions tend to occupy lower-energy spectral regions;
- vibrational transitions occur at higher characteristic energies;
- electronic transitions generally require still larger energy differences;
- sufficiently energetic photons can ionise atoms or molecules.

The exact response depends on the material, quantum selection rules, environment, coupling strength, temperature and other physical conditions.

The important point is not that “everything is frequency.”

The defensible statement is:

> **Physical systems possess characteristic modes and allowed transitions, and their response to external driving can depend strongly on frequency.**

---

## 5. Resonance and Selective Coupling

A resonant system responds disproportionately strongly when driven near one of its characteristic frequencies or transition energies, provided the relevant coupling is allowed.

This general pattern appears across many physical domains:

- mechanical oscillators;
- acoustic systems;
- electrical circuits;
- optical cavities;
- molecular rotations and vibrations;
- atomic electronic transitions;
- collective modes in condensed matter.

Resonance therefore provides a real mechanism by which the same external energy amplitude can produce very different outcomes depending on frequency and system state.

This does **not** imply that resonance lets macroscopic objects pass through ordinary matter. Nor does it imply that a frequency can simply “cancel” atomic solidity.

The microscopic structure of matter is constrained by quantum states, interaction potentials, conservation laws and enormous numbers of coupled particles. A resonance in one degree of freedom does not erase those constraints.

## 6. Why Ordinary Matter Does Not Pass Through Other Matter

The large geometric separation between nuclei does not create macroscopic passageways.

Two approaching objects bring vast collections of electronic states into interaction. Long before nuclei could freely interpenetrate, the combined system encounters substantial electromagnetic and quantum-mechanical resistance.

Quantum tunnelling does allow individual quantum systems to have non-zero probability of crossing barriers that would be forbidden classically. Tunnelling is experimentally established and essential in many technologies and physical processes.

However, extrapolating single-particle or few-particle tunnelling to a whole macroscopic person or object passing coherently through a wall is not physically realistic. The required coordinated quantum event across an astronomical number of particles has a probability so small that it is effectively zero for ordinary macroscopic conditions.

So the correct distinction is:

> **Microscopic quantum penetration is real. Macroscopic matter passing through ordinary matter because atoms are “mostly empty” is not a viable consequence of that fact.**

## 7. Electromagnetic Fields as Participants

Electromagnetic fields are already part of ordinary matter physics. Charged particles interact electromagnetically, and light itself is an electromagnetic field excitation.

That fact does not establish an independent electromagnetic memory field.

Within the current Verrell’s Law boundary, the disciplined position remains:

> **Fields may participate; retained state is primary.**

A field-based contribution would have to be demonstrated through measurable quantities, controlled perturbation, repeatable effects and predictive power beyond ordinary retained-state variables.

This note therefore treats electromagnetic fields as known physical participants in atomic and molecular behaviour, while leaving any stronger retained-state or memory claim explicitly unproven.

## 8. Relevance to Verrell’s Law

Verrell’s Law currently concerns whether retained prior state produces a measurable, directional and predictive influence on later selection under controlled conditions.

Atomic structure and resonance do not prove that claim.

They do, however, provide a useful conceptual comparison:

- apparently stable macroscopic behaviour can emerge from distributed microscopic interactions;
- a system’s response can depend on its present internal state;
- allowed transitions are constrained rather than arbitrary;
- coupling strength can depend on frequency and mode structure;
- the same external input can produce different outcomes in differently prepared systems.

These parallels are conceptual only unless a direct experimental mapping is demonstrated.

The correct research posture is therefore:

> **Established atomic and resonance physics may motivate controlled questions about substrate-sensitive retained-state coupling, but it cannot be used as evidence for Verrell’s Law without a defined observable and a successful experiment.**

## 9. Possible Experimental Questions

The useful next step is not to add new ontology. It is to formulate measurements.

Possible exploratory questions include:

1. In a system already known to retain state, does controlled periodic driving alter the strength or decay time of the retained-state effect?
2. Does that modulation show a reproducible frequency response rather than a generic heating, noise or energy-input effect?
3. Can a frequency-dependent change be predicted from independently measured properties of the substrate?
4. Does the effect disappear when the relevant mode is detuned, damped, erased or physically isolated?
5. Does any observed modulation change the Verrell’s Law retained-state coupling estimate in a preregistered and reproducible way?
6. Can ordinary confounds — temperature, stimulation energy, drift, instrument response, expectation, uncontrolled feedback or direct memory storage — fully explain the result?

A negative result would be informative. It would narrow the set of plausible substrates rather than damage the retained-state framework itself.

## 10. Claim Boundary

This note **does not claim** that:

- atoms are literally empty in the classical sense;
- matter can normally pass through other matter;
- resonance can remove macroscopic solidity;
- all physical behaviour is reducible to frequency;
- consciousness is an electromagnetic field;
- memory is stored in a universal or non-local field;
- atomic resonance proves Verrell’s Law;
- Collapse Aware AI proves a physical field mechanism;
- quantum tunnelling provides a plausible mechanism for macroscopic objects to pass through walls.

It records a narrower proposition:

> **Microscopic matter is governed by quantum states, electromagnetic interaction and frequency-dependent transitions; these established facts justify asking carefully controlled questions about whether resonant or field-mediated effects can modulate retained state in particular physical substrates.**

Nothing stronger is asserted here.
